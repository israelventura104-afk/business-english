Lesson 08: Writing a Service Recovery Email

You work in client success. A payroll file failed and the client is waiting for a written recovery plan. Use the right part of speech so the email is clear, precise, and professional.

Parts of speech Client success Word forms TOEIC word class

Scenario

Role: Client Success Specialist at OrbitPay, a B2B payroll platform.

Challenge: Friday’s payroll file for Greenfield Clinics failed. About 180 staff were not paid on time. Dana Cole, HR Manager, wants a written explanation and a recovery plan today. Your manager, Marcus Hale, will review the email before it goes out.

Goal: Choose the correct part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, and the linking words around them) so the recovery email sounds professional — not “almost English.”

Grammar Reference · Parts of Speech

A part of speech is the job a word does in the sentence. In recovery emails and TOEIC, the gap often needs the same idea in a different word class.

Content wordsNoun names a thing: apology, inconvenience, resolution
Verb shows action: apologize, restore, confirm
Adjective describes a noun: delayed, responsible, sincere
Adverb describes a verb or adjective: promptly, sincerely, immediately
Linking wordsPronoun replaces a noun: we, they, this
Preposition shows relation: regarding, during, by
Conjunction connects ideas: and, but, therefore, because
Determiner points to a noun: a, the, our, each, this
Quick testAsk: Does this slot need a thing, an action, a description, or a link? Then pick that class.

Same family, different job

apologize (verb) · apology (noun) · apologetic (adjective)

delay (noun/verb) · delayed (adjective)

resolve (verb) · resolution (noun)

confirm (verb) · confirmation (noun)

responsible (adjective) · responsibility (noun)

prompt (adjective) · promptly (adverb)

sincere (adjective) · sincerely (adverb)

restore (verb) · restoration (noun)

inconvenience (noun/verb) · inconvenient (adjective)

How the slot tells you the class

a / the / our + ______ → usually a noun: an apology · the resolution · our responsibility

We / They + ______ → usually a verb: We apologize · They confirm · We restore

a ______ + noun → usually an adjective: a delayed payroll · a responsible team · a sincere apology

verb + ______ → often an adverb: process promptly · apologize sincerely

Common mistakes

Not: We apology for the delay. → Correct: We apologize for the delay. / Please accept our apology.

Not: The salaries were delay. → Correct: The salaries were delayed.

Not: We will process the transfer prompt. → Correct: We will process the transfer promptly.

Not: Please send a confirm. → Correct: Please send a confirmation. / Please confirm.

Not: This is our responsible. → Correct: This is our responsibility. / Our team is responsible.

Reading · Manager Note on Your First Draft

Read Marcus’s internal note about your draft email. Then complete the text-completion items (same skill family as TOEIC Part 6).

ORBITPAY · Internal · Marcus Hale, Head of Client Success

Subject: Re: Greenfield Clinics — draft recovery email (rewrite before we send)

Hi,

Thanks for turning this around quickly. The commercial idea is right: a same-day emergency transfer for the 180 unpaid staff, a short cause (file failed validation), and a request for confirmation. Do not send this version to Dana yet.

The problem is word class, not the plan. “We apology for the delay” needs the verb apologize — or keep the noun and write “Please accept our apology.” “The salaries were delay” needs the adjective delayed. “We will process the transfer prompt” needs the adverb promptly. “Please send a confirm” needs the noun confirmation. Dana will forward this email to her CFO; sloppy forms make the whole recovery look weak.

Keep the facts we agreed: the file failed validation at 6:12 p.m.; operations is responsible for restoring the run; funds should land by 4:00 p.m. today. Promise a prompt update — or say we will update her promptly. Those are two different jobs in the sentence.

Regarding next steps, ask Dana to confirm receipt and offer a 15-minute call at 4:30 p.m. if any line is still missing. Rewrite the opening and send it back to me in 20 minutes.

Thanks,
Marcus

Script (teacher / self-study)

“Hi, it’s Marcus. I read the Greenfield draft. The plan is fine: a same-day emergency transfer for the one hundred eighty unpaid staff, and a fifteen-minute call at four thirty if anything is still missing. But do not send this version. You wrote ‘we apology for the delay’ — that has to be the verb, apologize, or keep the noun and write ‘please accept our apology.’ You also wrote ‘the salaries were delay.’ Delay is a noun or a verb. After were, use the adjective delayed. And ‘we will process the transfer prompt’ is wrong. Prompt is an adjective. After process, use the adverb promptly. One more: ‘please send a confirm’ needs the noun confirmation. Operations is responsible for restoring the run. Funds should land by four p.m. Regarding next steps, ask Dana to confirm receipt. Rewrite the opening and send it back to me in twenty minutes. Thanks.”
