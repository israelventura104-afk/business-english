Lesson 05: Project Meeting Debrief

You are the Logistics Coordinator at an automotive company. Report what went wrong in yesterday’s project meeting using the Simple Past — and pronounce regular -ed endings clearly.

Simple Past -ed sounds Automotive logistics Meeting debrief

Scenario

Role: Logistics Coordinator at AutoLine Components (automotive parts).

Challenge: Yesterday’s seat-foam project meeting went poorly — late start, incomplete updates, no clear owner. Today you must report what happened with accuracy and a professional tone.

Goal: Use the Simple Past for finished events and control the three pronunciations of regular -ed (/t/, /d/, /ɪd/).

Language focusSimple Past + -ed pronunciation
RecycledSimple Present (L04) for contrast: routines vs. what happened
OutputManager email / oral debrief of the meeting

In this lesson you will

  • Describe a difficult project meeting in the Simple Past
  • Sort and say regular past verbs with /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/
  • Find and correct Simple Past errors in a summary email
  • Extract facts from a debrief call and report to your manager

  • Started almost 15 minutes late; two suppliers missed the prep call
  • Only packaging shared a full update; foam delivery window stayed unclear
  • Production pushed back the trial run; no clear owner assigned
  • Only decision: a follow-up on Friday

Grammar Reference · Simple Past

Use the Simple Past for finished actions and situations in the past — perfect for meeting debriefs and incident reports.

AffirmativeSubject + past form
The meeting started late.
We discussed the bottleneck.
Negativedidn’t + base form
They didn’t assign an owner.
We didn’t agree on next steps.
QuestionsDid + subject + base form
Did packaging share an update?
When did the meeting start?

Time markers

yesterday · last week · after the call · in the end · at the start · during the meeting · an hour ago · on Monday

-ed pronunciation (regular verbs)

/t/After unvoiced sounds (p, k, f, s, sh, ch…)
missed · pushed · asked
/d/After voiced sounds / vowels
shared · raised · assigned · confirmed · emailed · flagged · arrived
/ɪd/After final t or d
started · noted · needed · interrupted · decided

Tip: Do not add an extra syllable on /t/ and /d/. finished is not “finish-ed-ed”.

Useful irregulars (mini set)

was / were · had · went · said · left · took · told · made

Example: The trial run was delayed. We had incomplete numbers on the dashboard.

Connection with Lesson 04

Simple Present: routines and facts now — We open the dock at 7:30 every day.

Simple Past: what happened once — Yesterday the meeting started late.

Common mistakes

Not: Yesterday we start late. → Correct: Yesterday we started late.

Not: Did they finished the update? → Correct: Did they finish the update?

Not: We didn’t assigned an owner. → Correct: We didn’t assign an owner.

Goals:
