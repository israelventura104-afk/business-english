Grammar Reference · Simple Present

Use the Simple Present for routines, facts, schedules, and regular operations in logistics.

Affirmative (3rd person)He/She/It + verb-s / -es
The driver leaves the depot at 10 a.m.
Negativedon’t / doesn’t + base form
The supplier doesn’t confirm the tracking number.
QuestionsDo / Does + subject + base form
Does the venue allow deliveries after 6 p.m.?

Frequency in operations

always · usually · often · frequently · every morning · every Tuesday · once a week

Example: Customs inspects every third container.

Connection with previous lessons

Indirect question: Could you tell me where the catering team meets?

First Conditional + Simple Present: If the shipment arrives late, we contact the forwarder immediately.

Common mistakes in ops emails

Not: The forwarder meet the window. → Correct: The forwarder meets the window.

Not: Does the venue allows late deliveries? → Correct: Does the venue allow late deliveries?

Not: The staff opens the dock at 7:30. → Correct: The staff open the dock at 7:30. (plural group noun in BrE/ops English)

As we approach the end of Q2, I would like to provide a comprehensive update on our current logistics performance. The freight forwarder meets 92% of our delivery windows this quarter. The security team handles the gates efficiently, but we recommend that they implement a digital logging system to reduce manual errors. Additionally, the catering team meets every day at 10 a.m. to coordinate the daily requirements. Could you please confirm whether the new exhibition stands already clear customs this week?
