Lesson 07: Drafting a Client Proposal

You work in sales. After a discovery call, you must draft a client proposal. Use the right part of speech so the writing sounds clear, precise, and professional.

Parts of speech Proposal writing Word forms TOEIC word class

Scenario

Role: Junior Account Executive at Helix Analytics, a B2B software company.

Challenge: After a discovery call with Tomás Herrera, Supply Planning Manager at Meridian Foods, your manager Priya Shah wants a short written proposal for a 12-week demand-forecasting pilot.

Goal: Choose the correct part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, adverb, and the linking words around them) so the proposal is client-ready — not “almost English.”

OutputA precise proposal paragraph and a follow-up email

Grammar Reference · Parts of Speech

A part of speech is the job a word does in the sentence. In proposals and TOEIC, the gap often needs the same idea in a different word class.

Content wordsNoun names a thing: proposal, client, implementation
Verb shows action: recommend, deliver, reduce
Adjective describes a noun: reliable, cost-effective
Adverb describes a verb or adjective: significantly, promptly
Linking wordsPronoun replaces a noun: we, they, this
Preposition shows relation: regarding, during, within
Conjunction connects ideas: and, but, therefore, because
Determiner points to a noun: a, the, our, each, this
Quick testAsk: Does this slot need a thing, an action, a description, or a link? Then pick that class.

Same family, different job

propose (verb) · proposal (noun) · proposed (adjective)

recommend (verb) · recommendation (noun)

reliable (adjective) · reliably (adverb) · reliability (noun)

implement (verb) · implementation (noun)

decide (verb) · decision (noun)

professional (adjective) · professionally (adverb)

significant (adjective) · significantly (adverb) · significance (noun)

prompt (adjective) · promptly (adverb)

How the slot tells you the class

a / the / our + ______ → usually a noun: a proposal · the implementation · our recommendation

We / They + ______ → usually a verb: We recommend · They deliver · We propose

a ______ + noun → usually an adjective: a reliable forecast · a cost-effective pilot

verb + ______ → often an adverb: reduce significantly · respond promptly

Common mistakes

Not: Please send a propose. → Correct: Please send a proposal.

Not: We recommendation a pilot. → Correct: We recommend a pilot.

Not: The system works reliable. → Correct: The system works reliably.

Not: We need a implement in October. → Correct: We need implementation in October. / We will implement it in October.

Not: This will significant reduce waste. → Correct: This will significantly reduce waste.

Reading · Manager Note on Your First Draft

Read Priya’s internal note about your draft proposal. Then complete the text-completion items (same skill family as TOEIC Part 6).

HELIX ANALYTICS · Internal · Priya Shah, Sales Manager

Subject: Re: Meridian Foods — draft proposal (need a rewrite before we send)

Hi,

Thanks for turning the discovery notes into a first draft so quickly. The commercial idea is right: a 12-week paid pilot in two distribution centers, with a weekly dashboard and a mid-pilot review. Do not send this version to Tomás yet.

The problem is word class, not the offer. “Please find our propose attached” needs the noun proposal. “We recommendation a pilot” needs the verb recommend. “The forecast will work reliable” needs the adverb reliably. “This will significant reduce waste” needs significantly. Tomás will read the first page in three minutes; sloppy forms make the whole offer look weak.

Keep the numbers we discussed: leftover stock is currently high, so a better forecast should significantly reduce waste. Implementation can start in the first week of October if they sign this month. Promise a prompt response to data questions — or say we will respond promptly. Those are two different jobs in the sentence.

Regarding price, keep the pilot fee on page two. End with a clear next step: a 20-minute call on Friday to confirm sites. Rewrite the opening and send it back to me this afternoon.

Thanks,
Priya

Script (teacher / self-study)

“Hi, it’s Priya. I read the Meridian draft. The offer is fine: a twelve-week paid pilot in two distribution centers, starting the first week of October. But do not send this version. You wrote ‘our propose’ — that has to be the noun, proposal. You also wrote ‘we recommendation a pilot.’ Recommendation is a noun. The verb you need is recommend. And ‘the forecast will work reliable’ is wrong. Reliable is an adjective. After work, use the adverb reliably. One more: leftover stock is high, so say the model can significantly reduce waste — significantly, not significant. Keep implementation on page one and the fee on page two. Regarding next steps, ask Tomás for a twenty-minute call on Friday to confirm the two sites. Rewrite the opening and send it back to me this afternoon. Thanks.”
