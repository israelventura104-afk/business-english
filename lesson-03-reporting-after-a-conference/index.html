Lesson 03: Reporting Back After an International Conference

You have returned from a logistics conference. Now you need to brief your manager on what happened, how people reacted, and whether the company should attend again.

Conference reportFeedback-ed / -ing adjectivesRecommendations

Scenario

Role: Logistics team member returning from an international conference.

Challenge: Report results clearly, describe people's reactions, and make a professional recommendation.

Goal: Use -ed and -ing adjectives naturally in a business debrief.

Manager opening line

"Welcome back. Was the conference useful enough for us to attend again next year?"

10 minWarm-up: event experience and business value.
15 minVocabulary cards for conference reporting.

-ing describes the thing

Use -ing adjectives to describe the event, presentation, problem, or situation that causes a feeling.

The keynote was interesting.

The booth location was disappointing.

The schedule was confusing.

-ed describes the person

Use -ed adjectives to describe how people feel because of the situation.

I was interested in the new platform.

The sales team was disappointed with the booth location.

Several attendees were confused by the schedule.

Business Debrief Pattern

The situation was + -ing, so people were + -ed. Then add the business result.

The panel was motivating, so our team felt motivated to improve follow-up material.

The low foot traffic was disappointing, but we were not disappointed with the quality of the leads.

interesting / interestedThe panel was interesting, so the managers were interested.
confusing / confusedThe schedule was confusing, and several attendees were confused.
motivating / motivatedThe success stories were motivating; our team felt motivated.
disappointing / disappointedThe low attendance was disappointing, but we were not disappointed with the leads.
surprising / surprisedThe number of questions was surprising; I was surprised by the interest.
challenging / challengedThe negotiation workshop was challenging; the new team felt challenged.

Do not write a short answer like "It was nice." Give your manager a useful business update. Use at least one -ing adjective for the situation and one -ed adjective for people's reaction.

1. Your boss asks if the panel discussion was useful. You want to say it gave the team two supplier contacts and clearer information about customs delays.
2. Your boss asks about the booth location. You want to explain that the location caused less foot traffic, but the team still collected strong leads.
3. Your boss asks if clients reacted positively to the tracking platform. You want to explain that the demo caused interest, especially from companies with cross-border shipments.

Self-check

Before you speak, check: Did you describe the situation with an -ing adjective? Did you describe people's reaction with an -ed adjective? Did you include a clear business result?

Include one positive result, one problem, one client reaction, and one recommendation.

Useful Frame

The conference was useful because... Some parts were challenging, especially... Clients seemed interested in... I recommend that we...

Post-Expo Stakeholder Feedback

Reading · -ed / -ing

Read the internal memo carefully. Then complete the comprehension questions and the -ed / -ing text completion task.

Reading passage · Internal memo

To: Mr. Henderson, CEO
From: Logistics Department
Subject: Post-Expo Stakeholder Feedback Report

The International Expo concluded last Friday, and we have collected feedback from all parties involved. Overall, the results are satisfying, though some areas require immediate attention.

The German supplier was pleased with the outcome. Their sales director said the event was more interesting than last year's conference in Berlin. However, the shipping team was exhausted after the first day. The delayed shipment on Thursday made the setup process extremely tiring, and several crew members felt completely drained by Friday morning.

The venue staff were mostly satisfied with our coordination. The head of security was particularly impressed by our organized approach to crowd control. That said, a few exhibitors were confused by the last-minute schedule changes. The new layout was somewhat confusing, especially for first-time participants who arrived on Saturday.

Our own team remains excited about next year's edition. The marketing lead found the networking sessions surprisingly productive, and the finance manager was relieved that we stayed within budget. Nevertheless, she was worried about the overtime costs accumulated during the final week.

One worrying trend emerged from the catering surveys: 30% of attendees found the lunch options boring and repetitive. The catering manager was surprised by this reaction, as he had been convinced the menu was exciting and diverse.

In summary, while the Expo was a successful and exciting milestone for the company, we must address the confusing signage and the tiring workload distribution before the next event. I am confident that with a few adjustments, the 2027 edition will be even more satisfying for everyone involved.

Listening Task

Listen to the debrief. The employee reports the conference results, explains people's reactions, and recommends whether to attend again.

Manager: Welcome back. Was the conference useful enough for us to attend again next year?

Employee: Overall, yes, but with some changes. The keynote was interesting, and many attendees were interested in our tracking platform, especially companies that manage cross-border shipments.

Manager: That sounds promising. Were there any problems?

Employee: Yes. The schedule was confusing, so some visitors were confused about our presentation time. Also, the booth location was disappointing because we had less foot traffic than expected.

Manager: Did we get any strong leads?

Employee: We collected eight strong leads and two possible supplier contacts. The most valuable insight was that clients want faster customs updates and clearer delivery notifications.

Manager: What about the sessions you attended?

Employee: The panel discussion was motivating because it showed that several companies have the same problem. I was also surprised by how much interest there was in automated tracking.

Manager: So what is your recommendation?

Employee: I recommend attending again, but only if we book a better booth, request a clearer presentation time, and prepare stronger follow-up material for leads.
